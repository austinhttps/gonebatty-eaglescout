Overview

The Project Mission

A look at the community need, our project objectives, and the beneficiary we served.

The Community Need

A 110-foot section of the Clearwater Nature Center's primary public trail was repeatedly flooded each spring, leaving the path covered in thick mud and standing water. This made the wetland corridor impassable for school field trips, families with strollers, and seniors, cutting off access to a key wildlife observation area.

Project Goal & Scope

My goal was to plan, fundraise, and lead the construction of a permanent, elevated wooden boardwalk with safe approach ramps and sturdy kickrails. This ensures year-round, dry, and safe accessibility while protecting the delicate wetlands underneath from soil erosion.

The Beneficiary

Clearwater Nature Center — A municipal non-profit dedicated to outdoor education, youth environmental programs, and public park stewardship in our community.

Site Condition Before Work: Flooded, muddy trail section prior to our build. Visitors were unable to navigate this area for nearly 4 months every year.

Milestones

Work Log & Project Phases

How the project progressed from an idea to completion.

1
Months 1 – 2

Proposal & Approvals

Met with the nature center director to agree on site needs. Wrote the official project proposal, drafted construction sketches, completed safety hazard assessments, and secured sign-offs from the beneficiary, Scoutmaster, Unit Committee, and District Advancement Committee.

2
Months 3 – 4

Fundraising & Materials

Held two troop car washes and arranged donor discounts with a local lumberyard to raise our entire budget. Prepared material lists (pressure-treated ground-contact timber, galvanized fasteners, foundation piers) and staged tools, first-aid equipment, and hydration stations.

3
Month 5

Execution Workdays

Directed 3 weekend work sessions. Formed specialized teams for excavation, joist framing, decking, and kickrail assembly. Conducted daily morning safety briefings and oversaw tool usage, ensuring youth and adult volunteers worked safely and efficiently.

4
Month 6

Final Inspection & Handover

Cleaned the work site and replanted native grass along the trail edges. Walked the boardwalk with the park director for final sign-off, completed the final project workbook documentation, and turned over the finished landmark to the community.

Experience

Leadership Reflection

Key takeaways on leading others, solving problems, and serving the community.

Leading Others, Not Just Doing the Work

The biggest lesson I learned from this project was the true meaning of leadership: my job was to direct, guide, and support my volunteers rather than try to build everything myself. On the first morning, I found myself grabbing a saw and doing manual labor while younger scouts waited for direction. I quickly stepped back, designated station leaders for excavation, cutting, and fastening, and focused on maintaining safety, keeping volunteers supplied with materials, and answering questions.

Problem Solving & Conflict Resolution

During our second workday, we encountered unexpectedly soft soil along a 20-foot stretch of the creek bank that threatened to sink our footing blocks. Instead of panicking or stalling progress, I gathered our adult technical advisors, called the park director, and worked out a solution: reinforcing the foundation with wider load-bearing sleeper sills and drainage gravel. This kept our timeline on track while keeping the structure safe and sturdy.

Working with Community Stakeholders

Balancing the needs of the city parks department, conservation board guidelines, troop adult leaders, and donor schedules showed me how critical clear and proactive communication is. Sending weekly email updates with photos and maintaining a transparent budget gave everyone confidence in the project.

"An Eagle Scout project isn't just about building a physical structure; it's about building your ability to bring people together for a common cause that benefits others for years to come."
